Melee Weapon Quality (p. 274), Silver Weapons (p. 275), Muscle-Powered Ranged Weapon Quality (p. 277), Ammunition (p. 279), Firearm Quality (p. 280), iron and plastic shields (p. 287), Equipment Modifiers (p. 345), and other rules in the GURPS library let you customize gear with modifiers. These aren’t the same modifiers as enhancements and limitations, or adjustments to dice rolls: They alter equipment stats like damage, range, and weight; adjust object DR, HP, HT, or breakage odds; or add unique effects.

Most modifiers multiply cash cost by a number, and it’s implied that when several of them apply, cost effects multiply together. This leads to weirdness like a bejeweled hilt multiplying cost by 20 for a good-quality sword but by 80 on a fine-quality one, because fine gives ×4 cost – adding the same hilt costs four times as much!

To remedy this, assign each modifier a cost factor (CF). If a modifier’s cost effects are worded as a multiple, CF is (multiple - 1); e.g., “×4 cost” is +3 CF, while “×0.4 of list price” is -0.6 CF. For percentages, divide by 100 first; e.g., “cost becomes 400% of list price” is +3 CF, while “40% of list price” is -0.6 CF. Where the effect adds a multiple of price, the multiple is the CF; e.g., “adds 3× price” is +3 CF. Again, divide percentages by 100 first, so “+300% to price” is +3 CF and “reduces price by 60%” is -0.6 CF.

To find final cost for something given several modifiers, multiply list price by (1 + total CF). For instance, for that bejeweled, fine-quality sword, read ×20 cost for jewels as +19 CF and ×4 cost for fine quality as +3 CF, giving +22 total CF and so ×23 cost. Total CF cannot fall below -0.8, no matter how bad an item is!

Any weight effects do multiply together; if “giant” gives ×4 weight and “unobtanium” gives ×10 weight, a giant unobtanium sword has ×40 weight. Adjustments to stats such as damage and DR add unless noted otherwise.

Modifiers are “stackable” except where noted, but many apply only to specific types of gear. Always read the description!

Some generic examples (specific supplements may redefine these):

Balanced: +1 to skill with any melee or thrown weapon, shield, or projectile (arrow, bolt, etc.), or +1 Acc with a muscle-powered missile weapon. All weapons except sticks and improvised weapons, and all shields: +4 CF.

Cutting-Edge\*: Made of the latest materials. Multiply weight by 2/3. Anything but armor or weapons (but can apply to laser sights, scopes, etc.): +1 CF.

Disguised: Resembles something else of similar shape; e.g., handcuffs that look like a chunky bracelet. Discovering the hidden function requires a Search roll. Any:

+4 CF.

Fine\*†: See pp. 274, 277, 280. Firearms: +1 CF. Projectiles (arrows, bolts, etc.), and crushing- or impaling-only melee or thrown weapons: +2 CF. Fencing weapons, swords, and muscle-powered missile weapons: +3 CF.

Other cutting melee or thrown weapons: +9 CF. Armor and shields can also be fine, multiplying weight by 3/4: +9 CF.

Fine-Quality Equipment\*†: See p. 345. If quality affects weight (adding more tools), ×20 weight. Anything but armor or weapons: +19 CF.

Good-Quality Equipment\*†: See p. 345. If quality affects weight, ×5 weight. Anything but armor or weapons: +4 CF.

Iron\*: See p. 287. Shields only (×2 weight, +3 DR, and ×2 HP): +4 CF.

Presentation: Modifies reactions from buyers and anyone impressed by such gear. If also disguised, decide whether the obvious or hidden item is pretty. Anything but sticks, improvised gear, or projectiles: +1 to reactions for +1 CF, +2 for +4 CF, or +3 for +9 CF.

Rugged\*: Shockproof and waterproof, giving +2 to HT and doubling the object’s DR. Multiply weight by 1.2. Anything but armor or weapons: +1 CF.

Silver†: See p. 275. Silver-coated/edged melee weapons and arrows: +2 CF. Silver-coated (“mirrored”) shields: +6 CF. Solid silver melee weapons and arrows: +19 CF. Solid silver bullets: +49 CF.

Very Fine†: See pp. 274, 280. Firearms: +4 CF. Fencing weapons and swords: +19 CF.

\* Weight effects multiply together; e.g., a cutting-edge, rugged camera has 0.8 times weight, and a fine iron shield has 1.5 times weight.

† Fine, very fine, and solid silver are mutually exclusive, as are fine- and good-quality equipment.
