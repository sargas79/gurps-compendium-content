**Variable**

Chapter 6 introduces psionic powers. Each is a collection of advantages that share an advantage origin (pp. 33-34), all with a power modifier that turns them into that power’s abilities by adding origin-specific behavior and associating them with a power Talent that facilitates their use. As Other Powers (p. 257) explains, powers needn’t be psionic! Possibilities are endless, but certain modifiers crop up often in GURPS and in fiction:

Biological: Abilities are physical capabilities of the body. They cost 1 FP per use to activate, and can be blocked or perverted by drugs, nanotech, pathogens, surgery, etc. -10%.

Chi: Abilities emanate from your life force. You must take a -10-point self-imposed mental disadvantage (p. 121), typically Disciplines of Faith or Vow, that requires daily exercise and meditation. Skipping a day means the entire power fails the first time you try to use any ability. Restoring it takes 1d days of rebalancing, during which the GM can inflict Irritating Conditions (p. 428) on you! -10%.

Cosmic: Abilities originate from the energy of creation! All attempts to negate, block, or neutralize them fail unless Cosmic themselves. This is identical to the +50% level of Cosmic on p. 103. Individual abilities may have more expensive forms of Cosmic, in which case this modifier covers the first +50%. +50%.

Divine: Abilities flow from a god. You must take a -10-point self-imposed mental disadvantage that suits your patron – often Honesty or Sense of Duty for a good entity, Intolerance or a heinous Vow for an evil one, or Code of Honor or Disciplines of Faith in general. Breaking faith costs you your power. Good deities spare you this until you’re out of danger, but require a month of penance to re-earn your powers; evil ones pervert your power horribly, but let you off the hook with a single act of darker-than-usual evil; most others fall somewhere in between, cutting off your powers immediately but not turning them against you, and requiring a minor quest to get them back. -10%.

Magical: Identical to Mana Sensitive, p. 34. -10%.

Moral: Abilities are manifestations of a general moral principle – Chaos, Evil, Good, Order, etc. You must take a -15-point self-imposed mental disadvantage associated with this cause, often Fanaticism. If you falter even briefly, you lose your power immediately. Regaining it requires you to recommit to your values for at least 2d days of lost time or earnings. Moreover, your guiding principle has an “opposite” (e.g., Evil opposes Good) whose effects, artifacts, and entities interfere with and sometimes neutralize your power. -20%.

Nature: Abilities channel the vital energy surrounding all living things. The ravages of civilization interfere with this, giving -1 to use your abilities in despoiled wild places (e.g., clear-cut forest), -3 in settlements, -5 amidst ordinary pollution, and -10 in poisoned wastelands. Where a penalty to dice rolls makes no sense, treat each -1 as a 10% loss of effect (advantage level, damage, reaction bonus, etc.). -10%, worsening to -20% if you’re also at a penalty equal to half the TL (round up) of the most advanced artifact you’re carrying, wearing, or riding in or on, or the full TL of any implants.

Psionic: The generic version of the ESP, Psychic Healing, Psychokinetic, Telepathic, and Psionic Teleportation modifiers on pp. 256-257. Use it for any power subject to the Antipsi power (p. 255) and anti-psionic technology (see GURPS Psi-Tech). -10%.

Spirit: Abilities come from spirits – you give orders and spirits do the work. You must take a -5-point self-imposed mental disadvantage, usually Disciplines of Faith or Sense of Duty (Servitor Spirits). If you fail to live up to this, the spirits deprive you of your power at the worst possible moment and refuse to listen to you until you make a minor sacrifice to them. -5%, or -25% if your spirits are also fickle and each use of any ability requires an unmodified reaction roll, with “Neutral” or better granting the desired effect but “Poor” or worse giving progressively more unpleasant perversions.

Super: Abilities stem from whatever “generic superpower origin” most of the game world’s power-wielders share. Special versions of Neutralize and Psi Static – and a bewildering range of weird-science plagues, rays, serums, etc. – work against them. In effect, this is an alternative form of Psionic. -10%.

Superscience: Abilities are generated by implausible genetic modifications, implants, or surgery that don’t drain the user like abilities with Biological do. When combined with gadget limitations (p. 116), they’re the effects of external artifacts. All are subject to just as many countermeasures as abilities with Super, although the two may be distinct if they coexist. -10%.
