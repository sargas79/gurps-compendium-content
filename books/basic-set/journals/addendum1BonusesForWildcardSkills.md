Wildcard skills (p. 175) strike some gamers as too costly for what they do. GURPS Power-Ups 7: Wildcard Skills offers many solutions, but here’s the simplest:

A positive relative skill level (p. 171) with a wildcard skill doubles as a bonus to all activities directly related to that skill.

Below are some examples, subject to GM fiat. Round all halved bonuses up.

- A bonus to rolls related to the wildcard’s high concept and to which no skill applies. A wildcard for inventors might help rolls on the Enigmatic Device Table (pp. 478-479) – and any wildcard could aid IQ rolls for learning, memorization, or recall of its subject matter, Will rolls for extra effort with it, and HT rolls to avoid FP losses to athletic feats it enables. When rolling fewer than three dice, like the 1d for initiative under Partial Surprise (p. 393), halve the bonus.

- A bonus to rolls to use conceptually related advantages; e.g., a wildcard for healers may add its bonus to rolls for the Healing advantage.

- A bonus to DX, IQ, HT, Will, or Per rolls to resist skills the wildcard encompasses (a social wildcard that covers many Influence skills could benefit Will rolls against those skills) or that are diametrically opposed to it (a wildcard for detectives might grant its bonus to penetrate deceptions involving Acting, Fast-Talk, Holdout, and Stealth). This benefit may extend to rolls to resist malign influences the wildcard studies: HT against poisons, resistance against supernatural attacks, and so on. Halve the bonus if it affects active defense rolls.

- A bonus to rolls to avoid bad effects encountered while using the wildcard; e.g., a nautical wildcard might benefit DX and HT rolls for rocking decks, seasickness, and similar shipboard hazards, and Will rolls to keep Aim in such an environment. This may encompass rolls to recover from failures and critical failures with the wildcard; e.g., a wildcard covering unarmed combat might benefit DX rolls to avoid falling after a missed kick. Halve the bonus if it affects active defense rolls.

- A bonus to reaction rolls made by or Influence rolls (p. 359) attempted on people who witness the user’s impressively broad skill – usually meaning “after a successful wildcard roll that benefits the target.” Halve the bonus if it affects a large group all the time (e.g., animals, for a beasttaming wildcard).

- An offset against penalties for circumstances relevant to the skill, particularly those under Equipment Modifiers (p. 345) and Geographical and Temporal Scope (p. 176).

- An Accuracy bonus when aiming relevant ranged weapons. The GM may restrict this to heroes with Gunslinger or Heroic Archer. Except in cinematic campaigns, this modifier is best halved.

- A ST or damage bonus when wielding relevant weapons. The GM may restrict this to heroes with Weapon Master, and might halve direct damage bonuses. For wildcards that replace skills that already give a bonus – like Brawling and Forced Entry – use this bonus instead of the usual one, but leave it open-ended.

- A bonus to HP restored, if the wildcard concerns healing or repair.

- A bonus to somebody else’s roll, when the wildcard directly aids others. Examples are a bonus to HT rolls to recover from crippling, disease, or injury treated by a medical wildcard, and a bonus to Fright Checks and self-control rolls for those led by a wildcard intended for a leader. When the wildcard is used for a complementary skill roll (p. 206), this is cumulative with the usual bonus!

A wildcard bonus can never “stack” with itself; e.g., it might work as a bonus to ST or damage with a melee weapon, but not to both at once.

For wildcard skills that include or have techniques – and techniques that are wildcards – see GURPS Power-Ups 7: Wildcard Skills.
