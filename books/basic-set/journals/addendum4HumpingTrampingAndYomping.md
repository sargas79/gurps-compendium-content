Whatever you call it, marching is exhausting. Hiking (p. 351) assumes a grueling 16-hour day spent doing nothing but putting one foot in front of the other . . . yet somehow averaging a marching speed approaching (2/3 × encumbered Move) miles per hour! Both day length and marching speed are optimistic. Here are more realistic figures.

Day Length: Subtracting 8 hours/day for sleep, 16 hours/day remain. If nobody in a group has a suitable skill for the non-hiking parts of the day – Professional Skill (Trail Guide), Soldier, Survival, etc., as determined by the GM – they need 1 hour to prepare (e.g., strike camp), 6 hours for hygiene, meals, rest breaks, etc., and 1 hour to end the day (e.g., pitch camp). They can hike for 8 hours/day. If even one person is skilled, pacing is better; these tasks take half as long, leaving 12 hours/day for hiking. Foraging (p. 427) takes an hour per attempt, subtracted from these times.

Marching Speed: A sustainable marching speed is (1/2 × encumbered Move) miles per hour. Adjust this as usual for terrain, weather, and roads. Thus, unskilled groups make 4 × Move miles/day in ideal conditions, skilled ones make 6 × Move miles/day, and anything better requires Hiking rolls, foregoing rest to recover FP (p. 426), or extra effort (p. 357), all of which work as usual.
