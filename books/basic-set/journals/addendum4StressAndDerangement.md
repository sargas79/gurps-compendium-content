Only a few results on the Fright Check Table (pp. 360-361) are lasting or permanent. Yet for realistic people, psychological trauma is as enduring as the physical kind – and in horror, the effects of encountering Things Man Was Not Meant To Know can be worse! Here are optional rules for campaigns in which the GM feels these matters merit the extra effort of tracking “mental FP” and “mental HP.”

In this system, there are two sorts of Fright Checks:

- Ordinary Fright Checks, for social anxiety, Phobias, dead bodies, artillery barrages, shocking revelations, most monsters and magic, and similar “routine” shocks that suit the game’s genre. Unfazeable grants total immunity. Failure causes the effects on the Fright Check Table and inflicts Stress (p. 573): Add -1 to Stress on a failure, -3 on a critical failure.

- Sanity-Blasting Fright Checks, for cosmic horrors, the darkest of black magic (like demonic possession), and technological interventions that shake the basis of the victim’s reality (like bad drugs and horrific brain surgery or implants). Unfazeable merely gives +8. Failure causes the effects on the table and inflicts Derangement (p. 573): Add -1 to Derangement on a failure, -3 on a critical failure.

The victim has a cumulative penalty to all Fright Checks of either kind equal to their current (Stress + Derangement)/2, rounded against them. While Stress and Derangement are “averaged” here, record them separately – they work differently in other respects.
