The GM may define minor, setting-specific social benefits as perks. Some examples:

Citizenship: If formal citizenship exists, everybody gets one citizenship for free. Each beyond the first is a perk.

Courtesy Title: You have an honorary social advantage; e.g., Courtesy Rank (p. 29), Emeritus Professor (for Tenure), Ex-Cop (for Legal Enforcement Powers), or Honorary Title (for Status). This lets you legally use a title and avoid skill penalties when trying to impersonate the real thing. It costs 1 point per 5 points the full advantage costs.

License: Licenses for regulated activities may carry modest social benefits set by the GM. These are perks with skill prerequisites: Law for lawyers, Physician for doctors, and so on. Where Status, Tenure, etc. are standard in a profession, take License only where it gives more leeway; e.g., Status 1 doctors have privileges that other Status 1 people don’t.

Office: A minor civil office may have real authority equivalent to 5 points of Legal Enforcement Powers, Security Clearance, Status, or similar – but of such limited scope (e.g., “Dogcatcher of the City of Austin”) that it’s only a perk.

Permit: If a piece of gear has a Legality Class (LC) lower than the setting’s Control Rating, legal carriage and use may require a permit for those without Legal Enforcement Powers, Rank, etc. (or even for such people when they’re “off the job”). Concealment can reduce effective LC for this purpose. Each such permit is a perk.
