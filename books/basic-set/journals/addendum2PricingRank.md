Next, assess the point cost per level of Rank in the organization. Rank that replaces a Patron typically costs the standard 5 points/level, but can range from 2 to 10 points/level, as the GM sees fit – or calculates using the optional rules in GURPS Social Engineering. The GM fine-tunes the cost to reflect the hypothetical Patron’s resources, legitimacy, and importance, respecting the ranges on the Patron-to-Rank Table and Rank-to-Patron Table (p. 338).

The hypothetical Patron might merit Highly Accessible (+50%) or Special Abilities (+50% or +100%), but Rank does not receive such modifiers unless the aid it brings or the accessibility of that assistance lets those with Rank function at a higher effective TL than the setting norm. In that case, increase point cost by the relevant percentage.

Example: Federal officers in the U.S.A. pay the usual 5 points/level for Rank, whether the year is 2025 (requests zip through fiber and satellites, help arrives in speedy aircraft), 1925 (requests go by wire, help comes by train or clunky auto), or 1825 (fast horses all around). That’s because everybody with Rank in those periods faces the same conditions. However, Rank in an organization that has satellites, telegraph, or horses – or telepathy! – in a world that generally doesn’t might cost 50% more. Rank in one with resources that would be “miraculous” in the setting (whether this means jets in 1925, cars in 1825, or magic in any era) should cost at least 50% more.

As for applying Patron’s limitations to Rank, Secret forbids the direct requests for aid that are the entire point of these rules, making it unsuitable.

Something akin to Minimal Intervention or Unwilling might fit an organization that places its needs ahead of its members’ emergencies. Treat such “Capricious Assistance” as a -50% limitation that reduces the cost of most Rank from 5 to 3 points/level. It means that only Assistance Rolls (p. 338) that succeed by 5+ summon the requested aid without complication. Success by 0-4 brings the desired help with a minor downside, or a benefit other than what was asked for. Failure by 1-4 brings nothing, or an unpredictable benefit outweighed by a major drawback. Failure by 5+ or critical failure spells disaster (“Agent Ripley requests assistance with aliens. Recommend nuking area from orbit.”).
