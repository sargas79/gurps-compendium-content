When PCs must roll many times, let them – players like to gamble, and to invoke Luck, Buying Success (p. 347), and similar rules. When it’s NPCs, shortcuts can avoid tedium.

Below, “target numbers” are relevant base scores adjusted for applicable modifiers. Treat a final, adjusted score of less than 3 as 3 here, to account for variation within the group and blind luck.

### How Many People?

For a question of how many people in a large group – say, 15 or more – succeed at a roll against the same target number, consult Probability of Success (p. 171), look up the target in “Skill Level,” and read “Probability of Success” as “Percentage Who Succeed.” Fractions are easier to work with, though. Use these approximations:

| Target | Fraction Who Succeed | Target | Fraction Who Fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 1/200 | 11 | 1/3 |
| 4 | 1/50 | 12 | 1/4 |
| 5 | 1/20 | 13 | 1/6 |
| 6 | 1/10 | 14 | 1/10 |
| 7 | 1/6 | 15 | 1/20 |
| 8 | 1/4 | 16 | 1/50 |
| 9 | 1/3 | 17 | 1/100 |
| 10 | 1/2 | 18 | 1/200 |

| Target | Fraction Who Succeed |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | 1/200 |
| 4 | 1/50 |
| 5 | 1/20 |
| 6 | 1/10 |
| 7 | 1/6 |
| 8 | 1/4 |
| 9 | 1/3 |
| 10 | 1/2 |
| Target | Fraction Who Fail |
| 11 | 1/3 |
| 12 | 1/4 |
| 13 | 1/6 |
| 14 | 1/10 |
| 15 | 1/20 |
| 16 | 1/50 |
| 17 | 1/100 |
| 18 | 1/200 |

Apply the fraction and round down to learn how many NPCs succeed or fail. The rest experience the opposite result. For a result less than 1 (e.g., 1/50 of 40), everyone really does fail or succeed!

This is useful for things like determining how many HT 11 commandos with Fit (+1 to HT rolls) succumb to poison gas resisted at HT-4. In that example, the target is 11 + 1 - 4 = 8, so 1/4 of them resist and 3/4 of them do not.

When resistance is a Contest (e.g., how many NPCs in a mob are fooled by Fast-Talk or affected by a Mass Daze spell), do this:

1. Roll once for the “attacking” skill, spell, or ability, accounting for all modifiers. If the feat is subject to The Rule of 16 (p. 349), apply whatever cap suits the subject group’s average resistance. If the “attacker” fails, they affect no one. Otherwise, record margin of success.

2. Subtract that margin of success from the subjects’ average resistance: Will for Fast-Talk, HT for Mass Daze, and so on. Treat adjusted resistance of less than 3 as 3.

3. Use adjusted resistance to find the percentage who succeed (resist) and fail (are affected) on the table above.

Example: A wizard casts Mass Daze-20 on a room full of orcs with average HT 12. The wizard is 2 yards from the edge of the area, for -2, giving skill 18 – but they’re limited to 16. The wizard rolls once and gets 10, succeeding by 6. Looking up 12 - 6 = 6 on the table shows that 1/10 of the orcs resist. The other 9/10 succumb!

### Collective Skill

If only one person in a big group must succeed – say, guards making Vision rolls to see something, sounding the alarm if they do – a different approach is needed. Start with the target number as before, but now look up the effective score for making many rolls on this table:

| Target | 5 | 10 | 20 | 50 | 100 | 200 | 500 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 14 |
| 4 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 11 | 13 | 16 | 16 |
| 5 | 8 | 9 | 11 | 14 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 6 | 9 | 11 | 13 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 7 | 11 | 13 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 8 | 12 | 15 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 9 | 14 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 10 | 15 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| 11+ | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 16 |

If the number of rolls falls between two standard values, use the lower. If the group is bigger than 500, effective score is 16.

For an uncontested roll, roll once against the collective score; e.g., when 6 guards are looking for the PCs, the largest applicable group size is 5, and with Vision 12 and -5 for darkness (7 or less), they have an effective 11.

For a contested roll, roll a single Contest using the collective score; e.g., if those 6 guards are looking for a PC with Stealth-14, roll a Quick Contest of their effective 11 vs. 14. This can be combined with Team Efforts (p. 185) on the PCs’ side.
