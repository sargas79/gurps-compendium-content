**5 or 10 points/level**

You plan so well that you can declare yourself to have taken retroactive actions. You may do this once per game session per level of Foresight. The action must be something you did in the past that has a specific result right now. There are limits on this:

1. It must be an action you could realistically have taken. If you’re whisked away to a secret headquarters for the first time, you cannot say that you’ve previously disabled the alarm system, because you’ve never been there and didn’t previously know where “there” is. You could say that you thought to bring along tools with which to do so now, though. You must also possess all of the skills and abilities necessary to have accomplished the task (see below), though you may rely on defaults, if applicable.

2. It cannot directly contradict established facts. If you’re being shot at, you cannot say that you decided to wear full body armor today – no one could have overlooked that! You can say that you put on a concealed Kevlar vest this morning, but only if you haven’t taken off your shirt recently. However, if you’ve been injured by previous shots, the “sudden reveal” of your vest doesn’t change your previous injuries! Reconcile this however you can (e.g., the previous bullets missed the vest); if you cannot, this isn’t a legal use of Foresight.

3. It cannot be obvious. No one who wasn’t involved can be aware of the action; its effects must lie in wait, unnoticed, until this very moment. So, you cannot retroactively attack a foe or speak with them, because this would contradict established facts from the foe’s point of view. For indirect interaction (e.g., picking someone’s pocket), the GM should make your roll (see below) in secret, as a Quick Contest against your target’s Per or an appropriate skill. If you win, you acted unnoticed; otherwise, either you failed or they saw what you did and can now turn the situation around to their own benefit, using your Foresight against you!

Some actions are automatic; e.g., it requires no roll to remember to tuck a handcuff key under your tongue. For others, the GM should require whatever roll or rolls would be necessary. (Indirect interaction always demands a roll of some sort; use DX or IQ if nothing else fits.) If they feel that you’re trying to do too much as a single “retroaction,” they can either forbid it or penalize every roll involved (-2 per “extra action” is fair). If the roll fails, your planning fell through; this still counts as a use of Foresight.

Any consequences of the action are paid for now, not in the past. (This may not be realistic, but it’s the only way to prevent certain kinds of abuse, as well as to keep the GM sane.) If you cast a spell in the past, you lose the FP now; if you entered a room full of toxic gas, the cyclic damage begins now; if you bribed someone, you must pay the money now; and so on.

With GM permission, you can involve helpers in your past action. Each person must have been present at the time and able to act. The limitations above apply to everyone. Only voluntary help is possible – tricking an unsuspecting person into having assisted you in the past would be an action all on its own!

The 10-point version of this trait allows you to take any retroactive action, subject to GM approval and the limits above. The 5-point version is limited to a specific type of action or actions pertaining to a specific situation, and should be written as, e.g., “Foresight 1 (Ambushes) [5].” Suitable examples include (but aren’t limited to): Ambushes: Being ready for surprise attacks. Backup: Calling friends in advance for assistance. Getaways: Stashing vehicles, unlocking doors, etc. Gizmos: Bringing along useful gear. Loyalty: Anticipating betrayals, lies, and moles. Research: Creating in-depth dossiers, maps, psych evaluations, etc. Survival: Preparing for acts of God and forces of Nature. Swaps: Trading one thing for another when no one’s looking.

The GM decides how much Foresight each player can take. A suggested cap is no more than 15 points spent on any combination of Foresight. For example, a “big brain” might take Foresight 1 [10] + Foresight 1 (Research) [5], while the ex-soldier could have Foresight 3 (Ambushes) [15].
