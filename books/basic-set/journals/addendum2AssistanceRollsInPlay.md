Someone in a Rank-granting organization can request its aid at any time, not just once at the start of an adventure. But there are conditions attached!

The petitioner must be able to contact their backer in a manner that suits their abilities, the organization, and the setting, especially its TL. They might visit in person; use smoke signals, drums, or horns; send a letter, runner, or pigeon; communicate by telegraph, phone, radio, email, etc.; or exploit extraordinary gifts (like Telesend). Depending on the options available, being stranded, imprisoned, behind enemy lines, and so on could prevent this – if it can’t, Rank should cost more. Regardless, being incapacitated means being out of luck unless the GM decides that someone of that Rank rates a search party when overdue . . . a kind of “involuntary AR.”

To get aid from an organization, the petitioner must also be in good stead with it:

- They must hold Rank, if only Rank 0, in that organization.

- If Rank requires a Duty or another disadvantage that imposes obligations, they must possess and honor those traits.

- They must be working to promote the organization’s cause – or at least, not working against it. The GM can use the appropriateness or inappropriateness of previous AR as a measure of this. Someone who abuses their privileges won’t stay in good stead for long!

If all conditions are met, the Rank to use for the AR is:

- For a lone petitioner (the usual case), that person’s Rank.

- For a request by a group that serves the same organization, the group’s highest Rank. If they’re numerous enough to merit a leader who has higher Rank than any of them, use their highest Rank+1.

- For anyone acting on behalf of an NPC who has preauthorized the aid requested, that NPC’s (usually higher) Rank. If the GM prefers, such aid is automatic – don’t roll for it!

Roll 3d against the number on the Assistance Rolls Table (p. 339) that matches this Rank, applying any relevant modifiers: In-world appropriateness of request: +1 to +5 if especially appropriate (most requests for preauthorized aid count), -1 to -10 if especially inappropriate (e.g., even a Military Rank 8 general has -10 to request a nuclear strike in most circumstances). Meta-game appropriateness of request: +1 to +5 if especially appropriate (granting the request would improve the story), -1 to -10 if especially inappropriate (granting the request would short-circuit the story). Innate modifiers for type of assistance: Some types of assistance are easier or harder to get than others; see Sample Assistance (p. 339). Preauthorized aid can sometimes waive penalties here. Previous requests: -1 per AR after the first made by the individual requesting aid or their team during the current adventure, or – on a mission for the organization – the current assignment. If preauthorized aid led to skipping a roll, don’t count it. Charisma and Smooth Operator: Treat their levels as bonuses for requests made person-to-person (only). Reputation: Add its level, positive or negative, if it’s specifically with the organization as a result of service record. Complementary skill roll (p. 206): The GM may allow one roll, giving the usual modifier, against Administration – or possibly Diplomacy, Politics, Savoir-Faire, etc., if that suits the organization.

An AR is a success roll. Luck and Buying Success (p. 347) can affect it. If the final, modified target number is less than 3, no attempt is possible – don’t roll dice or count it as a previous request.

**Success or Critical Success:** Brings the specific aid requested.

**Failure:** Brings no aid.

**Failure by 10+ or Critical Failure:** Brings no aid – and if the request rated a penalty for in-world inappropriateness, it also triggers disciplinary action.
