Physical labor often requires the combined strength of several people. Think of a work gang as a single large, strong creature – and for some jobs, such a creature, usually a draft animal, might replace it. But you cannot simply add ST scores!

In most cases, performance depends on Basic Lift (BL). To find this for a group, add the workers’ BL scores. For instance, Basic Lift for the average man is 20 lbs., so BL for 10 men is 200 lbs. (If you added ST for those men, you’d get ST 100, giving an obviously excessive BL 2,000 lbs.!)

You might sometimes need an effective group ST. If so, this is the square root of (5 × total BL), rounded up to the next whole number. For instance, effective ST for those 10 average men is the square root of (5 × 200), or ST 32. If everybody has the same ST, you can multiply average ST by the square root of the number of workers and round up – it gives the same result (e.g., ST 10 times the square root of 10, or 3.16, also gives ST 32).

These calculations work for teams of draft animals, too.
