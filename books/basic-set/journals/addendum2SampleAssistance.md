Here are examples of assistance that PCs with Rank might ask backers for. Few organizations grant all or even most of these things! The type of Rank involved is a good indicator; e.g., diplomats with Administrative Rank can’t call in air-strikes, while soldiers with Military Rank can’t expect bailouts justified by diplomatic immunity.

If the requested aid makes sense, use the power level under The Organization (p. 337) to gauge how impressive a successful AR is. For demands for anything more substantial than information, treat the organization as a Patron (p. 72) with assets appropriate to its power level. It is possible for a request to be appropriate to the backer and the Rank they bestow, but also beyond their resources to make happen – a local cop can say, “I’d like the country’s top forensics labs to examine this!”, but their supervisor may be unable to do better than a wistful “So would I.”

This list is by no means exhaustive. The GM is encouraged to create custom lists for specific groups in their game world.

**Any Assistance Available!** Beg for help and let the organization decide how to respond! Make an unmodified AR. Success means the GM sends the aid they feel is appropriate – the larger the margin of success, the more helpful this is.

**Bailout:** When legitimate authorities or “honorable” enemies detain someone with Rank while they’re following orders, the detainee can try an AR to “get out of jail free” if their organization could reasonably retrieve them by paying a fee (bail, ransom, etc.) or overruling their detention. Time required is as little as 10 minutes after a phone call on home turf, but possibly several days otherwise.

**Cash:** Unlike most assistance, this scales with Rank: up to 0.5% of campaign starting money at Rank 0, 1.5% at Rank 1, 5% at Rank 2, 15% at Rank 3, 50% at Rank 4, 150% at Rank 5, 500% at Rank 6, 1,500% at Rank 7, or 5,000% at Rank 8. Multiply by 10 if it’s for show and will be returned. Cash can be collected in person, delivered by courier in a day, or – in high-tech worlds – deposited electronically. AR is at -4 for an effective 10-point Patron, no modifier for a 15-point Patron, +2 for a 20-point Patron, +4 for a 25-point Patron, or +6 for a 30-point Patron.

**Consultation:** If the PC puts a question to their organization or asks its specialists to carry out a task, treat it as a Contact Group (p. 44) with an effective skill of 15 + (equivalent Patron value/5) – i.e., from 17 to 21 – in whatever skill seems applicable. A successful AR allows a skill roll, at that skill’s usual modifiers, with any success giving the petitioner what they need to know or have done. Reliability is “Somewhat Reliable”: On a failed skill roll, the information or service just isn’t available. On an 18, someone decides that the request was excessive, and instead of aid, investigators complicate the adventure.

**Cover-Up:** Hiding the fact that something unpleasant ever happened. Look at every other task on this list that would be required to make this happen, and then make one AR at the worst penalty.

**Disappearance:** “Authorization” to make an illegal arrest. The individual(s) asking must do the dirty work, but a successful AR guarantees them holding facilities and no legal trouble. For setup time, use the guidelines for Warrant. AR is at -5 if target isn’t an enemy of the state, known terrorist, or similarly scary bad guy.

**Entry Clearance:** Permission to enter physical premises – other than the petitioner’s home base – controlled by the organization. AR is at +5 unless it’s a secret base.

**Evacuation:** Physically and overtly plucking the petitioner(s) from danger (contrast this with Insertion/Extraction). High-tech help like a helicopter or an ambulance arrives in 1d+10 minutes, but 1d hours or worse is typical at lower TLs or in remote areas. AR is usually at from +1 to +5 for appropriateness, and another +5 if the hopeful beneficiary is injured.

**Facilities:** Access to the best-quality computer, lab, machine shop, etc. The equipment bonus (p. 345) on a successful AR equals 1/5 of effective Patron value – from +2 (at 10 points) to +6 (at 30 points) – but cannot exceed the usual +TL/2. It can be had only in person, on home territory, for facilities that make sense; e.g., city cops can access an auto shop but not code-breaking supercomputers.

**False ID:** A temporary identity (p. 31). Usually ready in one day – but versions with life histories, supporting documents, etc., can take weeks.

**Files:** Any dossiers, manuals, maps, etc. that the organization would believably possess. Collect in person, take electronic delivery in 1d minutes, or wait a day for a courier.

**Fire Support:** Airstrike, artillery strike, naval or orbital bombardment, etc., starting 1d minutes after the call – or instantly, if preplanned. AR is at -5 outside a combat zone or -10 in friendly territory!

**Gear:** Any equipment that’s standard issue for the organization and the petitioner’s role in it. Collect it in person or wait a day for a courier (up to 1d days for big items like vehicles). Military personnel can sometimes get an airdrop in just 1d hours.

**Generalized Assistance:** If the GM can’t place the aid into a clear category, treat the AR as a complementary skill roll (p. 206) to rolls to resolve the situation that triggered the AR, justified by bribes, clues, helpers, or whatever else fits. Critical success at the AR gives a bonus equal to 1/5 of effective Patron value, from +2 (at 10 points) to +6 (at 30 points); success gives half that (round down); failure gives -1; and critical failure gives -2.

**Insertion/Extraction:** Clandestine drop or pickup by getaway car, helicopter, submarine, etc. Time varies from 1d+10 minutes for loitering support to a day or more if called in cold.

**Introduction:** Most organizations can provide introductions that get their members past their affiliates’ gatekeepers. This doesn’t guarantee success or a good reaction at the endeavor – it just means the person who makes the AR gets the necessary interview. AR is at +5 for an introduction to members of the same organization!

**Invitation:** The petitioner’s name on the guest list for a social event tied, often indirectly, to their organization. AR is at +5 to “jump the queue” of a public event, or waived for an invitation the PC would normally receive anyway.

**License:** Authority to undertake an activity or use certain equipment (vehicle, weapon, etc.) that requires permits. AR is at +5 if the organization is the usual issuer of such certifications! For equipment, apply a penalty equal to local CR and a bonus equal to item LC.

**Muscle:** “Persuasive gentlemen,” with a headcount equal to at most half the equivalent Patron value (e.g., 5 people at 10 points), arrive quietly in 1d+10 minutes – or in 1d hours, away from home base. They have any relevant skills, particularly combat skills, at level 10-15 (1d+9), and light civilian weapons (daggers, cudgels, handguns, etc.). These aren’t the petitioner’s usual subordinates from Rank, and may have an agenda.

**Records Search:** A search of all databases to which the organization has access, in order to dig up everything it has regarding a piece of information – e.g., name, address, license number, phone number (TL6+), or DNA sample (TL8+) – provided by the petitioner. Treat this as Consultation, but the relevant skill is automatically Research. Collect the results in person, take electronic delivery in 1d minutes, or wait a day for a courier.

**Safehouse:** The archetypal safehouse is unknown to the opposition, checked for traitors (if staffed) and bugs (at higher TLs), and stocked with food and medical supplies. If the AR succeeds, the address is provided immediately via the same channel – but the building might be 3d miles away in town, or in the nearest big city if the hero is in the countryside.

**Shipping:** The organization takes care of shipping a fragile or sensitive package, or one that requires tricky paperwork. At TL6+, this can often be arranged (almost) in real time by telegraph or telephone; at lower TLs, it may take several days of waiting for suitable horse couriers, merchant ships, mail trains, and the like. For dodgy items, apply a penalty equal to local CR and a bonus equal to item LC.

**Technical Means:** Communications intercepts for a specific target, plaintext of a code broken on a supercomputer, drone or satellite recon of a locale, etc. AR often has an innate -1 to -5 for expense and complexity. Most requests take a day or more to process – and another day or more to travel by courier, if electronic communication isn’t possible.

**The Cavalry:** Similar to Muscle, but the arrivals come loaded for bear and barge onto the scene noisily. Headcount is still half the equivalent Patron value – the local sheriff’s department (10 points) might have a five-man SWAT team, while a modern army (20 to 25 points) would send a 10- to 12-man squad. These reinforcements are on duty, making appropriateness a concern – AR is at -5 or worse for frivolous requests.

**Transportation:** Ordinary, overt transportation, complete with relevant papers (tickets, visas, etc.). The organization, not the traveler, chooses the means! At TL7+, this can often be arranged in real time over the phone; at lower TLs, it may take several days.

**Warrant:** A legal arrest, search, or surveillance warrant. Arrangements take 1d hours at day but 1d+8 hours at night (fewer judges!) – unless lawyers get involved, then it’s weeks. AR is at up to +5 for a target “known to the authorities,” but down to -5 if the petitioner has a hunch but no evidence.
