Disadvantages can take limitations. Some disadvantages in Chapter 3 have special limitations, and Mitigator (p. 112) is a general limitation designed specifically for disadvantages. This doesn’t mean that freely applying limitations to disadvantages is a good idea – often, directly adding general limitations produces unbalanced results! To illustrate, consider two characters:

Ada has IQ 10 [0] and IQ +2 (Accessibility, Only during the day, -20%) [32], for 32 points.

Bob has IQ 12 [40] and IQ -2 (Accessibility, Only at night, -20%) [-32], for 8 points.

Both have IQ 12 during the day and IQ 10 at night, but Bob has spent a fraction of what Ada has. That’s because most limitations have modest values; a trait losing 50% of its utility might save only 20% of its value. With positive traits, like attribute increases and advantages, this helps prevent abuse. With negative traits, like attribute reductions and disadvantages, it can actually facilitate abuse.

Thus, for the most part, general limitations (aside from Mitigator) shouldn’t be applied to disadvantages. Instead, the best option is to take the disadvantage at full value, and then take an advantage that counters the disadvantage to apply any limitations to.

Example: The counter to Deafness [-20] would be No Deafness [20]. So, rather than take Deafness (Accessibility, Not in direct sunlight, -10%) [-18], it’s fairer to take Deafness [-20] + No Deafness (Accessibility, Only in direct sunlight, -30%) [14], for a net -6 points.

Though complex, this method produces fair results. To keep things simpler, the GM can figure out what limitations would be applied to the “counter” advantage, subtract those from -100%, and treat the result as a special limitation for the disadvantage.

Example (continued): No Deafness (the “counter”) ended up with a -30% limitation. -100% minus -30% is -70%; thus, rather than writing out both traits on the character sheet, the GM could tell the player to treat “Not in direct sunlight” as a -70% limitation here. It works out the same: Deafness (Not in sunlight, -70%) [-6].
