Divide base hypothetical Patron cost by per-level Rank cost and drop fractions to find the Rank roughly equivalent to a Patron that appears on 9 or less.

Examples: A small-town police department is a base 10-point Patron and Police Rank there costs 4 points/level; 10/4 = 2.5, so Rank 2 marks the level where a cop gets help on 9 or less. The FBI is a base 20-point Patron, and its Police Rank costs the standard 5 points/level; 20/5 = 4, so Rank 4 is the level where an agent gets help on 9 or less.

Appearance rolls based on Rank – called Assistance Rolls (AR) – fan out to either side of this key level. Each Rank above that level gives +1 to AR; each Rank below that level gives -2. The Assistance Rolls Table (p. 339) summarizes this for the recommended combinations of Rank and Patron value. Don’t treat numbers less than 3 as “impossible” (or 15 as “automatic”), as modifiers often apply to AR.

Again, remember that the PC doesn’t buy a Patron! The Patron is totally hypothetical. Still, it’s useful to record equivalent Patron power and base AR with Rank on character sheets.

Examples (cont’d): A small-town cop might list Police Rank 2 (Town PD; 10-point Patron; AR 9) [8]. An FBI officer might note Police Rank 4 (FBI; 20-point Patron; AR 9) [20]. These advantages are roughly equivalent to Patron (Fairly powerful organization; 9 or less) [10] and Patron (Very powerful organization; 9 or less) [20].
