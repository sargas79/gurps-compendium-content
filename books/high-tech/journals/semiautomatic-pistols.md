The semiautomatic, or self-loading, pistol is a TL6 innovation that gradually replaces revolvers and all other handguns – although some older patterns continue to see service, especially in specialty niches. Early semiautomatics are somewhat unreliable, but after the teething problems are resolved, many of these pistols offer supreme reliability. Their main advantages over revolvers are greater ammunition capacity and faster reloading. They can also be made more compact.

From late TL6, and especially at TL7-8, semiautomatic pistols are the weapons of choice for the military. They gradually replace revolvers in the hands of police and law-abiding citizens, too. By TL8, revolvers are mostly confined to specialty uses and private shooters.

Semiautomatic pistols can be single-action, double-action, double-action-only, or safe-action; see Trigger Mechanisms (p. 82).
