Guns that use compressed air to propel a projectile technically aren’t “firearms” at all, but don’t significantly differ from them in rules terms. The first examples are hand-built custom models dating to the early 17th century (mid-TL4). Mass-produced hunting and military air rifles appear at TL5.

For most of TL5, the limitations of loose powder and shot mean that it’s easier to build a repeating air gun than a black-powder repeater – despite the fact that air reservoirs are unreliable and successive shots are at progressively lower velocities owing to loss of pressure. At late TL5, the metallic-cartridge repeater usurps the air gun’s unique status as a multi-shot weapon impervious to humidity and rain. Air guns retain the advantages of flashless and comparatively quiet firing, however.
